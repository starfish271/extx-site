    text: The Process
  - type: paragraph
    body: |-
      1. Wrap yarn around book about 20 times
      2. Wrap a piece of yarn around a pencil and tape to secure, make sure its around twice as long as your book width.
      3. Pull the other end of thread underneath the first warp thread, and wrap it back around the pencil.
      4. Skip the next thread and repeat step 3, repeating this over every other thread.
      5. With a popsicle stick, push it under the second thread, skip a thread, then under the next, repeating over every other thread, so that half of your threads are underneath the popsicle stick, and every other is underneath the pencil with thread.
      6. Now you can lift up the pencil to create a shed to push your shuttle through, and lift up the popsicle stick to create the other shed.
      7. Note that the setup should look like what is pictured above, the pencil/thread contraption should be below the popsicle stick.
      8. Another tip: Weaving in a popsicle stick near the bottom of the book keeps the warp threads more even.

  - type: heading
    text: Reflection
  - type: paragraph
    body: This project was a really great way to understand how looms help with the weaving process and how weaving works in general. My finished swatch was a navy blue and red plain weave. Since this was my first time weaving, I had some trouble with tension so it started caving in a bit in the middle. I also ran into issues with the warp threads on the sides getting woven too tightly together. I want to vary this book loom technique by making evenly spaced slits to hold the warp yarn in place. Overall I really enjoyed doing this and now I can replicate this if I want to make a quick swatch in a simple and portable way.
