  - type: paragraph
    body: Before I got started with weaving, we made 2 different coils by wrapping magnet wire around a pencil/straw about 200 times. This was a lot more challenging than I originally thought it would be. The magnet wire slipped off the straw really easily, but stayed on better when wrapped around a pencil or something faceted. The first coil's wire broke when I got to 130 wraps, so I started working on another. Getting the coil off the pencil and tying it with a string was also kind of difficult. After ruining the coil a couple times from taking it off the pencil, I eventually started wrapping the wire around the straw, feeding a string through the center of the straw, and then taking the coil off and tying the string together.
  - type: paragraph
    body: I was really inspired by this tangled mess of yarn and while detangling it I cut off a few knots that I eventually turned into flowers and wove the ends into the textile, then sewed them on to secure in place.

  - type: paragraph
    body: After sewing and weaving everything in, we connected the coils to a power source to make the beads flip. I really enjoyed incorporating the magnet wire into the weave and watching the beads flip. If I were continue working with flip beads, I think it would be cool to make a grid of them on a piece of fabric, have different colors for the two sides, and program them to flip at certain times and make patterns or animations from it.
