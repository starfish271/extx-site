    text: Getting started
  - type: paragraph
    body: |-
      These are all the materials you may need:


      Paracord: 30" (cut into 3 equal pieces)


      Silicone Coated Wire: 30" (cut into 3 equal pieces)


      Shrink Tubing: 9" (6, 1.5" pieces)

      Quilt Clips: 4

      Conductive Tape: 12" (cut into smaller pieces)

      Multimeter: 1

      (optional) Glue Gun: 1

      This instruction manual (from [https://extx.unstable.design/docs/weeklies/2-make-connectors/](https://extx.unstable.design/docs/weeklies/2-make-connectors/)) is really helpful to reference while buidling the connectors:
      [https://ireneposch.net/clipprobe-diy/](https://ireneposch.net/clipprobe-diy/)
  - type: paragraph
    body: Once you have gathered all materials and cut paracord to the desired length, you should first take out the insides of the paracord. Then tape around your clips following the intructions. I connected the magnet wire to one of the clips, and fed the other end through the empty paracord center. Then I put the paracord/magnet wire through 2 small pieces of shrink wrap, and connected the other end of the magnet wire to the other taped up clip. I then put the shrink wrap in place and put it over some heat from a lighter to secure the shrink wrap around the clip and paracord, then same thing with the other side. It was really important to remember to add both shrink wrap pieces before securing the other end of the magnet wire to the 2nd clip.

  - type: heading
    text: Reflection
  - type: paragraph
    body: This project was fascinating to me because it was like making a fabric jumper wire. In some of my other classes I do prototyping with jumper wire on breadboards, and the paracord/magnet wire combo kind of reminded me of a solid core wire. I want to experiment more with different clips/probes, and am excited to learn more about how I could incorporate something like this in my work with electronics and also textiles.
